English Major📚

I started as an Inventory Control Specialist at Loma Linda University Hospital: Children's Operating Room.

Before my work as an Inventory Control Specialist, I worked as a Perioperative Tech and was in charge of crating case carts that were efficient enough for getting cases ready for surgical procedures. Doing this work was extremely eye opening, as I was able to look into the process of an operation, on kids, that saved their lives from some of the craziest forms of health hazardous complications. It was with great honor that I helped the clinical staff with the issues thta arose from being a level one trauma hospital within this region. Working alongside this staff was intriguing, though new as my main source of experience in the working field consisted of retail management and retail management assistance. This experience enabled me to do serious critical thinking when it came to the cases, as well as manage my time in some of the most beneficial ways. With the increase of critical thoughts, I was able to determine what and where I was supposed to be in the event of many different emergencies. Unfortunately, this does mean that I have witnessed some awful deaths, in which are so extremely sad. However, this helped shape my experience both personally and professionally by numbing myself to many of the awful circumstances that we see in everyday life. It’s not to say we experience death in everyday life, but it does give you a greater appreciation for the things that we do currently contain while we are living.

While I continue to live, I decided to pursue my education in English for the reasons of becoming a teacher. Unfortunately, overtime, my will to want to teach has gone down-hill and I had to quickly reform my thoughts and plans for my future career. Now that I am working in healthcare, I decided to shift my thoughts and vision toward becoming something higher in human resources for healthcare administration. Although I must confess, I didn’t expect to be something like that and don’t quite know what to expect in it anymore. I am deeply grateful for the opportunity to pursue and complete my English degree, an achievement that represents far more than academic success alone. Studying English has allowed me to develop not only stronger communication and analytical skills, but also a greater understanding of people, culture, and the power of language itself. Throughout this journey, I have been supported by dedicated professors, encouraging peers, and loved ones whose guidance and belief in me made this accomplishment possible. I recognize the privilege of receiving an education and the responsibility that comes with it, and I do not take either for granted. Earning this degree has strengthened my confidence, broadened my perspective, and inspired me to continue learning long after graduation. Above all, I am thankful for the experiences, challenges, and opportunities that shaped me into the person I am today, and I look forward to applying what I have learned in meaningful and impactful ways.
